This revision closes three source-to-claim gaps without treating adjacent evidence as direct support.

## `strong_03` — acute sleep loss

The prior wording said sleep was repeatedly restricted, while both syntheses primarily evaluated acute sleep loss. The prompt now specifies substantial sleep loss during the preceding night and the population explicitly names acute loss.

Sources:

1. Craven J, et al. “Effects of Acute Sleep Loss on Physical Performance: A Systematic and Meta-Analytical Review.” *Sports Medicine*. 2022. DOI: `10.1007/s40279-022-01706-y`; PMID: `35708888`.
2. Gong M, et al. “Effects of Acute Sleep Deprivation on Sporting Performance in Athletes: A Comprehensive Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.” *Nature and Science of Sleep*. 2024;16:935–948. DOI: `10.2147/NSS.S467531`; PMID: `39006249`; PMCID: `PMC11246080`.

The direction is strongly supported; task, timing, and sleep-loss protocol remain effect modifiers.

## `strong_05` — strength-specific creatine synthesis

The body-composition meta-analysis was removed because it did not directly map to the strength outcome.

Replacement:

- Wang Z, et al. “Effects of Creatine Supplementation and Resistance Training on Muscle Strength Gains in Adults <50 Years of Age: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.” *Nutrients*. 2024;16(21):3665. DOI: `10.3390/nu16213665`; PMID: `39519498`; PMCID: `PMC11547435`.

This synthesis directly evaluates upper- and lower-body strength gains with creatine plus resistance training. The existing ISSN position stand remains the second source for efficacy, safety, and population boundaries.

## `strong_07` — suspected exertional heat stroke

The previous phrase “symptoms compatible with heat illness” covered conditions too broad for the heat-stroke sources. The prompt now fixes two central emergency indicators during strenuous exercise in heat: altered mental status and collapse.

Sources:

1. Roberts WO, et al. “ACSM Expert Consensus Statement on Exertional Heat Illness: Recognition, Management, and Return to Activity.” *Current Sports Medicine Reports*. 2023. DOI: `10.1249/JSR.0000000000001058`; PMID: `37036463`.
2. Belval LN, et al. “Consensus Statement—Prehospital Care of Exertional Heat Stroke.” *Prehospital Emergency Care*. 2018. DOI: `10.1080/10903127.2017.1392666`; PMID: `29336710`.

The revised decision is immediate cessation, rapid cooling, and emergency activation. It does not encode a universal cooling duration or substitute for local emergency protocols.
