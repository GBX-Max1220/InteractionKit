## Why the adult technique item was replaced

The previous item generalized a technique-before-load rule across adult novices and complex exercises. Its sources were broad resistance-training progression statements rather than direct guidance for the stated technique decision. Keeping the item would have confused a reasonable safety heuristic with a source-validated binary ground truth.

## `strong_11` — supervised progression for an adolescent novice

New decision context: a healthy adolescent novice cannot perform a resistance exercise with stable technique at the current load in a supervised program.

Provisional preferred decision: use a manageable load with qualified instruction until technique is consistent rather than increase load and attempt to correct technique during heavier repetitions.

Sources:

1. Faigenbaum AD, et al. “Youth resistance training: updated position statement paper from the National Strength and Conditioning Association.” *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*. 2009;23(5 Suppl):S60–S79. DOI: `10.1519/JSC.0b013e31819df407`; PMID: `19620931`.
2. Lloyd RS, et al. “Position statement on youth resistance training: the 2014 International Consensus.” *British Journal of Sports Medicine*. 2014;48(7):498–505. DOI: `10.1136/bjsports-2013-092952`; PMID: `24055781`.

Both position statements directly address youth resistance training, qualified instruction or supervision, technique, individual capacity, and gradual progression. The revised item does not generalize the recommendation to unsupervised adults or prescribe one numerical load threshold.
