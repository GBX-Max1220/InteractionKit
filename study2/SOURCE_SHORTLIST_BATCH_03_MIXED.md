## Advance to full dossier

### `mixed_01` — concurrent-training order with strength priority

1. Eddens L, et al. “The Role of Intra-Session Exercise Sequence in the Interference Effect: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis.” *Sports Medicine*. 2018. DOI: `10.1007/s40279-017-0784-1`; PMID: `28917030`.
2. Petré H, et al. “Development of Maximal Dynamic Strength During Concurrent Resistance and Endurance Training in Untrained, Moderately Trained, and Trained Individuals: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.” *Sports Medicine*. 2021. DOI: `10.1007/s40279-021-01426-9`; PMID: `33751469`.

Decision: advance. Resistance-first is supported when strength is prioritized, while training status, outcome, and scheduling define the boundary. Full metadata and the exact order-specific effect must be verified in the dossier.

### `mixed_02` — cold-water immersion during hypertrophy training

1. Piñero A, et al. “Effects of post-exercise cold-water immersion on resistance training-induced gains in muscular strength: a meta-analysis.” *European Journal of Sport Science*. 2022. DOI: `10.1080/17461391.2022.2033851`; PMID: `35068365`.
2. Yu T, et al. “Cold water immersion protocol optimization across exercise modalities: a systematic review and network meta-analysis of resistance training, endurance exercise, and team sport applications.” *BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation*. 2026. DOI: `10.1186/s13102-026-01653-5`; PMID: `41845491`.

Decision: advance. Recovery benefits and possible chronic adaptation costs make the recommendation goal- and timing-dependent. The current “normal week/no urgent competition” boundary is essential.

### `mixed_04` — static versus dynamic warm-up before sprint/jump tasks

1. Behm DG, et al. “Acute effects of muscle stretching on physical performance, range of motion, and injury incidence in healthy active individuals: a systematic review.” *Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism*. 2016. DOI: `10.1139/apnm-2015-0235`; PMID: `26642915`.
2. Zheng Z, et al. “Effects of post-activation performance enhancement added to general warm-up on jump, sprint, and change-of-direction performance in competitive athletes: a systematic review and meta-analysis.” *BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation*. 2026. DOI: `10.1186/s13102-026-01758-x`; PMID: `42226251`.

Decision: advance with replacement-source request. Source 1 directly compares stretching modes; source 2 concerns a different active warm-up mechanism. Retrieve a second direct static-versus-dynamic synthesis before completion.

### `mixed_06` — failure versus near-failure hypertrophy training

1. Grgic J, et al. “Effects of resistance training performed to repetition failure or non-failure on muscular strength and hypertrophy: A systematic review and meta-analysis.” *Journal of Sport and Health Science*. 2022. DOI: `10.1016/j.jshs.2021.01.007`; PMID: `33497853`.
2. Refalo MC, et al. “Influence of Resistance Training Proximity-to-Failure on Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis.” *Sports Medicine*. 2023. DOI: `10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y`; PMID: `36334240`.

Decision: advance. The syntheses do not support taking each set to momentary failure as categorically superior; load, volume, fatigue, and failure definition remain important boundaries.

### `mixed_07` — one-minute versus three-minute strength rest

1. Grgic J, et al. “Effects of Rest Interval Duration in Resistance Training on Measures of Muscular Strength: A Systematic Review.” *Sports Medicine*. 2018. DOI: `10.1007/s40279-017-0788-x`; PMID: `28933024`.
2. de Salles BF, et al. “Rest interval between sets in strength training.” *Sports Medicine*. 2009. DOI: `10.2165/11315230-000000000-00000`; PMID: `19691365`.

Decision: advance. Longer rest often supports volume and strength in heavy multi-joint work, but time constraints, training status, and exercise selection prevent a universal interval claim.

### `mixed_11` — caffeine-sensitive evening athlete

1. Guest NS, et al. “International society of sports nutrition position stand: caffeine and exercise performance.” *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition*. 2021. DOI: `10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4`; PMID: `33388079`.
2. Gardiner C, et al. “The effect of caffeine on subsequent sleep: A systematic review and meta-analysis.” *Sleep Medicine Reviews*. 2023. DOI: `10.1016/j.smrv.2023.101764`; PMID: `36870101`.

Decision: advance. Performance benefit, dose-response variability, timing, sensitivity, and sleep cost make this a defensible conditional decision.

### `mixed_12` — compression garments when comfort is primary

1. Brown F, et al. “Compression Garments and Recovery from Exercise: A Meta-Analysis.” *Sports Medicine*. 2017. DOI: `10.1007/s40279-017-0728-9`; PMID: `28434152`.
2. Li X, et al. “Effects of Compression Garments on Muscle Strength and Power Recovery Post-Exercise: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.” *Life*. 2025. DOI: `10.3390/life15030438`; PMID: `40141783`.

Decision: advance with outcome clarification. The current prompt specifies comfort, while source 2 emphasizes strength/power. Add a soreness/comfort-specific synthesis or change the outcome before completion.

### `mixed_15` — planned hydration versus thirst

1. McDermott BP, et al. “National Athletic Trainers' Association Position Statement: Fluid Replacement for the Physically Active.” *Journal of Athletic Training*. 2017. DOI: `10.4085/1062-6050-52.9.02`; PMID: `28985128`.
2. Hew-Butler T, et al. “Statement of the Third International Exercise-Associated Hyponatremia Consensus Development Conference, Carlsbad, California, 2015.” *Clinical Journal of Sport Medicine*. 2015. DOI: `10.1097/JSM.0000000000000221`; PMID: `26102445`.

Decision: advance. Both underdrinking and overdrinking create risk; event duration, weather, sweat loss, pace, and hyponatremia risk determine whether a plan or thirst-led strategy is preferable.

### `mixed_16` — high-dose antioxidant supplementation

1. Pastor R, Tur JA. “Antioxidant Supplementation and Adaptive Response to Training: A Systematic Review.” *Current Pharmaceutical Design*. 2019. DOI: `10.2174/1381612825666190701164923`; PMID: `31267859`.
2. Chen J, et al. “Effects of different antioxidants on exercise-induced oxidative stress and muscle damage in athletes: a systematic review and meta-analysis.” *BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation*. 2025. DOI: `10.1186/s13102-025-01381-2`; PMID: `41219818`.

Decision: advance. Effects differ by compound, dose, deficiency status, acute recovery outcome, and chronic adaptation goal. The prompt must retain “healthy athlete with adequate diet” and avoid generalizing across all antioxidants.

## Hold: no defensible binary ground truth yet

### `mixed_03` — protein within 30 minutes versus next meal

Sources retrieved: Zhou et al. 2023 network meta-analysis (`10.1123/ijsnem.2023-0118`; PMID `38039960`) and Casuso & Goossens 2025 direct-timing meta-analysis (`10.3390/nu17132070`; PMID `40647175`).

Decision: hold. With daily needs met and a protein-containing meal two hours before exercise, both options may be acceptable. A mixed evidence label does not create a uniquely correct answer. Revise to a context in which delay is meaningfully longer or reject.

### `mixed_05` — foam rolling versus progressive rehabilitation

Sources retrieved: Skinner et al. 2020 (`10.1016/j.jbmt.2020.01.007`; PMID `32825976`) and Wiewelhove et al. 2019 (`10.3389/fphys.2019.00376`; PMID `31024339`).

Decision: hold. These syntheses concern range of motion, performance, soreness, and recovery in healthy participants; they do not validate foam rolling against progressive rehabilitation for an unspecified injury. Name a condition and rehabilitation target or reject.

### `mixed_08` — fasted versus preference-based aerobic sessions

Sources retrieved: Vieira et al. 2016 acute-metabolism meta-analysis (`10.1017/S0007114516003160`; PMID `27609363`) and Aird et al. 2018 performance/meta-analysis (`10.1111/sms.13054`; PMID `29315892`).

Decision: hold. Acute fat oxidation is not long-term fat loss, and the prompt does not identify a uniquely correct schedule under matched energy and exercise. Replace with an acute performance decision or reject.

### `mixed_09` — soreness versus program outcomes

Candidate evidence addresses soreness mechanisms and recovery interventions, not validity of soreness as a program-effectiveness measure.

Decision: hold. The contrast is intuitively educational but not yet grounded as a binary decision in two authoritative syntheses. It is also vulnerable to an obvious-answer ceiling.

### `mixed_10` — HIIT versus MICT as a beginner default

Source retrieved: Guo et al. 2023 (`10.3390/ijerph20064741`; PMID `36981649`) plus multiple modality comparisons.

Decision: hold. Both modalities can improve fitness, and “default” cannot be adjudicated without a specified outcome, dose, supervision, preference, or risk profile. Revise to one concrete constraint or reject.

### `mixed_13` — two versus four weekly hypertrophy sessions

Sources retrieved: Schoenfeld et al. 2019 (`10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906`; PMID `30558493`) and related frequency syntheses.

Decision: hold. Under volume matching, the evidence suggests little meaningful hypertrophy difference, so neither schedule is uniquely correct absent scheduling or recovery constraints. Revise around a stated constraint or reject.

### `mixed_14` — mild non-specific low-back pain

Sources retrieved: Oliveira et al. guideline overview (`10.1007/s00586-018-5673-2`; PMID `29971708`) and Rodríguez-Domínguez et al. resistance-training meta-analysis (`10.1080/09638288.2025.2566275`; PMID `41065407`).

Decision: hold for population repair. Evidence supports activity/exercise, especially for chronic non-specific low-back pain, but the current prompt does not state acute versus chronic duration. Add a chronic-duration boundary and preserve red-flag exclusion before dossier completion.
